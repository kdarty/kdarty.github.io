Doctor Who Halloween Watchlist (Scariest Episodes)

Doctor Who has only ever built one episode around Halloween itself — 2021's "The Halloween Apocalypse," set on the night — so this watchlist leans instead on the stories fans and critics reliably call the show's scariest, from the Weeping Angels to a gas-masked child to a Doctor trapped alone in a shifting castle. U.S. streaming is split by era: the 2005–2022 episodes are on AMC+ (also available as an add-on through Prime Video, Apple TV, and Roku), the 2023 Ncuti Gatwa era is on Disney+, and the classic serials live on BritBox, with a free, ad-supported selection on Tubi. Ratings are each episode's IMDb score — two-part stories show both parts, and the classic multi-part serials show the serial's average. (U.S. availability current as of 2026.)

Episode Synopsis

The Empty Child / The Doctor Dances

The Ninth Doctor and Rose land in London during the Blitz and run into a small boy in a gas mask who keeps asking "Are you my mummy?" Anyone he touches ends up with the same mask fused to their face. It was Steven Moffat's first script for the show and a lot of fans point to it as the moment the revival figured out how to be scary.

The Impossible Planet / The Satan Pit

The TARDIS arrives at a base on a planet that shouldn't be able to orbit a black hole. The crew's servant creatures start speaking with a voice that claims to be something ancient, maybe the Devil himself. It's the closest the show gets to straight-up cosmic horror.

Human Nature / The Family of Blood

To hide from a family of alien hunters, the Doctor turns himself human and lives as a schoolteacher in 1913 England with no memory of who he is. The Family finds him anyway, and they bring an army of walking scarecrows. It's creepy, and it's honestly pretty heartbreaking too.

Blink

Sally Sparrow finds messages from the Doctor hidden under wallpaper and on old DVDs, and they lead her to the Weeping Angels. They're statues that only move when nobody's looking at them, and a touch sends you back in time. The Doctor barely appears, which somehow makes it work even better. This is the one people usually name first.

Silence in the Library / Forest of the Dead

The Doctor and Donna visit a library the size of a planet that's been silent for a century. The Vashta Nerada live in the shadows, and they'll strip a person to the bone in seconds. This two-parter also introduces River Song.

Midnight

The Doctor takes a sightseeing shuttle across a diamond planet where nothing is supposed to live. Something knocks on the hull, then one passenger starts repeating everything everyone says. There's no monster suit at all. The fear comes from the passengers turning on each other, and it's arguably the most unsettling Tennant episode.

The Waters of Mars

In 2059 the Doctor arrives at humanity's first base on Mars, right before a disaster history says kills everyone there. A water-borne infection turns the crew into cracked, dripping zombies. The ending pushes the Doctor somewhere pretty dark.

Night Terrors

A frightened boy's plea for help reaches the Eleventh Doctor, who shows up with Amy and Rory at a council flat. People in the building get pulled into a dollhouse and turned into giant wooden peg dolls. It's a family-friendly one, but those dolls stick with you.

Listen

The Twelfth Doctor becomes obsessed with the idea of a creature that's perfectly hidden, the thing you feel is there when you're alone. He and Clara chase that fear across time, and there's one scene with something under a bedspread that people still argue about. The episode never quite tells you whether the monster was real.

Heaven Sent

Grieving and alone, the Doctor is trapped in a shifting castle while a slow, veiled figure stalks him. It only stops when he confesses a truth. This is basically a one-man show for Peter Capaldi and many critics rank it among the best episodes the series has made.

The Haunting of Villa Diodati

The Thirteenth Doctor and her friends drop in on Mary Shelley and Lord Byron on the stormy 1816 night that led to Frankenstein. The house keeps rearranging itself, ghosts wander the halls, and a severely damaged Cyberman is on its way. It's a proper haunted-house story, shot largely by candlelight.

The Halloween Apocalypse

This is the one actual Halloween episode. On October 31 a destructive force called the Flux begins tearing through the universe, and an ancient enemy escapes his prison. The Doctor meets Dan Lewis in present-day Liverpool, and the Weeping Angels show up too. It's more of a busy action opener than a scare-fest, but the date alone earns it a spot.

73 Yards

The Doctor steps on a fairy circle on the Welsh coast and vanishes, leaving Ruby alone. After that a strange woman stands exactly 73 yards away from her, always, for years. Anyone who goes up to the woman and hears what she says runs from Ruby in terror. It plays like folk horror and it doesn't hand you tidy answers.

The Dæmons

A 1971 story with the Third Doctor. An archaeological dig in an English village opens an ancient barrow on the night of a pagan festival, and the Master is secretly running a local coven. A horned being called Azal wakes up, plus a stone gargoyle that comes to life. It's pure 70s occult horror, a bit camp, which honestly makes it great October viewing.

The Ark in Space

The Fourth Doctor finds humanity's survivors asleep aboard a space station in the far future. An insect species called the Wirrn has gotten there first and plans to use the sleepers as food and hosts. Body-horror scenes like a man slowly turning into an insect were pretty wild for a 1975 family show.

The Brain of Morbius

Another Tom Baker story, and a clear nod to Hammer horror. On a storm-lashed planet, a mad scientist is building a patchwork body to house the brain of an executed Time Lord criminal. There's a castle, a cult of Sisters, and a Frankenstein-style monster. It's probably one of the more gruesome stories from the classic era.
